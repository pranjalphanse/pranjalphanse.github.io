What I do

I've spent six years at Upstox, one of India's largest retail investing platforms, as the analyst sitting between raw platform data and the people making decisions on it.

Most of the work falls into three buckets. Figuring out where users drop off and why — funnels, cohorts, retention, churn. Making sure the numbers are right before anyone acts on them — reconciliation, validation, anomaly detection. And removing the manual work that eats analyst time, so there's room left for actual investigation.

The part I care about most is the second one. A dashboard nobody trusts is worse than no dashboard, and a plausible-looking number that's quietly wrong is the most expensive thing in analytics. I'd rather chase why a figure moved than ship a chart that says it did.

Selected work

Reconciliation and detection logic

Problem
Transactional charges weren't reconciling against expected delivery. Individually small enough to look like rounding, which is why nobody had flagged them.
Constraint
No reliable ground truth to validate against.
Approach
Pulled a longer window and found the pattern was systemic, not random. Quantified frequency and what the false-positive rate would be if we built detection on it — a noisy alert is worse than none. Documented thresholds and edge cases, then shipped the detection logic with engineering rather than handing over a notebook.
Outcome
Stopped ongoing revenue leakage, and the same break is now caught automatically instead of found by hand.

Intraday funnel monitoring

Problem
Acquisition performance was only visible the next morning, so decisions about spend were always a day behind.
Approach
Built hour-by-hour tracking across six channels through the full funnel — lead through verification to activated account — with day-over-day and week-over-week comparison, plus written commentary so readers got an interpretation rather than a wall of numbers.
Outcome
Same-day optimisation instead of next-day reporting. It also surfaced a mid-funnel conversion decay isolated to specific hours and specific traffic, invisible in daily aggregates, which redirected spend.

Activation funnel diagnostic

Problem
Multi-step onboarding was losing users, but the aggregate numbers didn't show where.
Approach
Stepped through the journey stage by stage and by channel to isolate where the drop actually happened, then traced it to cause rather than stopping at the observation.
Outcome
A 15% improvement in activation once addressed. I then turned the finding into a live monitoring framework tracking leading indicators, so the same class of issue gets caught in flight.

Reporting automation, 25 datasources

Problem
Twenty-five recurring extracts were being run by hand every week, and the manual steps introduced errors of their own.
Constraint
Replacing the stack wasn't on the table, and the outputs had to keep landing in the same places people already worked.
Approach
Built one modular SQL and Python architecture reading from a versioned query folder, scheduled and delivering into Sheets and Slack — rather than a new tool or a pile of one-off scripts.
Outcome
Recurring reporting effort down about 25%, a persistent class of manual error gone, and analyst time freed for investigation work.

Propensity model, derivatives users

Problem
Outreach was undifferentiated — everyone got contacted the same way regardless of how likely they were to engage.
Approach
Built a Gradient Boosting model scoring users by likely engagement and frequency, owning the feature engineering, validation, and post-deployment monitoring. Made the case for the approach in business terms before building it.
Outcome
Prioritised outreach in place of blanket contact, with the model's performance tracked after launch rather than assumed.
